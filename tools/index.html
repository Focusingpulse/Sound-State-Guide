Tools and Programs

If you already understand the basics and the Dr. Jeffrey Thompson lineage, the next question is simple: which tool or program fits your goal?

This page is here to help you compare the main options without getting overwhelmed.

What kinds of tools are in this ecosystem?

The Thompson ecosystem includes a few different kinds of experiences.

1. Audio programs

These are track-based sound experiences you can listen to directly.

They are usually the simplest way to start if you want a low-friction entry point, something portable, or something you can test on your own.

2. Vibroacoustic tools

These use sound and vibration through a physical device, like a chair or table.

They may fit better if you want a more immersive experience, sound you can feel as well as hear, or a more embodied use of the method.

3. Personalized sound work

This is a more tailored approach, often tied to Bio-Tuning or HRV-based feedback.

It may fit better if you want a customized experience, a practitioner-guided path, or something beyond generic audio tracks.

4. Training and certification

This layer is for people who want to learn the method more deeply.

It may fit better if you want formal learning, practitioner development, community access, or a more structured path through the ecosystem.

How to choose

A good choice starts with your goal.

If your goal is relaxation

Start with the simplest audio option.

If your goal is sleep support

Look for the most beginner-friendly, low-friction program.

If your goal is focus or work support

Choose a track or program that is easy to use consistently.

If your goal is a deeper session

A vibroacoustic or practitioner-guided option may make more sense.

If your goal is to learn the method

Training may be the right lane.

What each option is best for

Audio programs

Best for first-time users, simple routines, home listening, and low cost or low friction entry.

Vibroacoustic tools

Best for a fuller body experience, sessions with more physical feedback, practitioner-style use, and people who want a stronger sensory experience.

Personalized sound work

Best for people who want more customization, nervous system-informed sessions, and a more tailored approach.

Training / certification

Best for learners, practitioners, and people who want to understand the method in depth or may want to use it professionally.

What to keep in mind before buying

  • What am I actually trying to support?
  • Do I want something simple or something more immersive?
  • Do I want self-directed or practitioner-led?
  • Do I want general sound support or something personalized?
  • Am I buying because it fits my goal, or because it sounds impressive?

That last question matters more than people think.

Our recommendation for beginners

If you are new, start small.

A simple audio program is often the easiest entry point. It gives you a way to test how sound affects your state without a bigger commitment.

If that feels useful, you can always explore more advanced or more personalized options later.

Final takeaway

Not every tool in the Thompson ecosystem is meant for the same person or the same goal.

That’s a good thing. The point is not to buy the most complex option. The point is to choose the one that fits your need, your comfort level, and your level of experience.

Start simple. Pay attention. Move up only if it earns its place.
